After leaving Christianity, I've become more and more disturbed by the doctrine of hell. While I once accepted it as fair and just punishment, I now cannot see how it would be compatible with the Christian god. I have two major reasons for thinking this, which I will explain in detail.


The first is about punishments in general. Why do we do them? For instance, when someone is put into prison after committing a crime, why did we put them there? There are a number of reasons to give, with the main one being to prevent future harm. By being incarcerated, they won't be able to harm those in the public. Or maybe it's so that they can be rehabilitated, and re-enter society as a productive citizen. Or maybe it will discourage others from doing the same thing. With these situations, we subject someone to punishment with the end goal of something good. We justify the negative of someone being in prison with the good of the public being safer.

There are often people who want criminals to be punished solely so that they suffer after causing harm to others. When someone has hurt us, we feel that they should be hurt back. It's important though to ask the question "who/what does this help?" Often this desire for criminals to suffer does lead to a good - the ones mentioned above. But what if it doesn't? What if we make someone suffer because they did wrong, and there is no good that comes of it? Would that not be cruelty?

This takes us back to the idea of hell. Hell does not resemble our justice systems. The eternal punishment of hell does not offer any positives or goods. No one can be rehabilitated or improve, because they are stuck there forever. No one is protected, because they are already dead. It also does a poor job at actual discouragement, because we can’t see and confirm that Hell is real. If we could all view those suffering in Hell, it would be much more effective. There simply is no good that it provides. While our punishments that we give each other are simply a means to an end, Hell is just suffering with no end goal. Hell is just cruelty, needless suffering.

Now, I do know about the idea in Christian theology that we deserve suffering or punishment because of our sins. Supposedly this justifies the cruelty because god just has to do this, basically as a law of nature. I personally don't think this makes any sense. I don't understand what it means for someone to 'deserve suffering' at all. If it doesn't help anyone, why would we do it?

I think a lot of this comes down to the way our brains are wired. I like to think of it as 'evolutionary proto-morality'. Someone hurts us, we want to hurt them back. Tit for tat. It serves as a very base-level intuition for justice. By having this ingrained in us by evolution, we can function societies as we are able to discourage bad behavior, and understand ourselves that our negative actions will have repercussions. The issue is, this is not the best way to discourage bad behaviour. Hurting those who hurt us just perpetuates violence, and we can see that everywhere. We also know from studies that rehabilitation is more effective than, say, simply locking people away. While evolution gave us this intuition that does work, it's far from the best. You'd think though that the all-knowing god would be aware that it's ineffective. Presumably he'd be aware of a system even better than rehabilitation. Curiously, however, god seems to match our imperfect, faulty intuition, as if he was created in our image instead of us in his.

To conclude this section, it appears that hell is simply a needless cruelty, which can provide no benefit. If god were all-loving, would he not create a system without this? At the very least he could annihilate those who would go to hell, instead of having them suffer forever. If god is unable to do this, he can't be all-powerful. If he chooses not to, he can't be all-loving. The best explanation this fits this is simply that god is cruel at the very least, or evil at worst.


The second important point is about the whole free will issue. Many Christians have said that god doesn't send people to hell, they send themselves to hell. By sinning against god, we are subjecting ourselves to hell and have no one else to blame. The only issue with this is that it does not work at all.

Supposedly, god has revealed himself to everyone (Romans 1:20) and it is up to us whether or not to accept god's revealed message. The issue with this is that god is all-knowing. He knows before he created anyone who would choose him, and who would not. He knows exactly what it would take to convince each person. He in fact creates people intentionally knowing that they will suffer forever in hell because they won't choose him. These are simply unavoidable facts if you agree that he is all knowing. The interesting thing is, god sometimes chooses to provide the required evidence to convince people. For instance, Saul (later Paul) was convinced because god directly intervened in a way that would convince him. God could do this for every single person if he wanted to, and have no one go to hell. Why doesn't he? Well, some say that god wants to give us free will. He provides some evidence to us, and it's our choice what to do with it. If we choose to ignore it, it's on us and is not god's fault. I don't think this works at all, and I'll give an analogy to explain what I mean.

Suppose you are inside your house, and you look out your window and notice your young child playing on the street. This street is very busy, and is not a safe place for a child to be playing. As the parent, you ought to go do something. Now, you could stand on your front step and shout at your child to come back in, or you could go out, grab them, and bring them back in. Let's say though that this kid is not good at listening. Maybe it's that they hear and don't care to listen, or maybe they just don't process it because they're in their own little world. In any case, you know, for sure, that if you call out to them they will not get off the street. If, knowing this, you still just stand there and call to them instead of rushing out to safe them, and they get hit by car, who is at fault? Well, you could claim that you called out and the child didn't listen, so it's their fault. But you did this knowing *with certainty* that they would not listen, when instead you could have ran out and grabbed them. You are to blame for this. You intentionally did not save them, when you could have. The fact that you called out does not absolve you, because you did this knowing it would not work. You chose for them to get hit by the car by intentionally not getting them off the busy and dangerous road. Now, legally this wouldn't get you in trouble for murder (maybe negligence), but if you think about who caused this to happen, who is at fault, I think it's pretty clear.

Let's bring this back to god now. If god is calling out to us in such a way that we won't notice, and he knows that it won't work, AND he could directly intervene (vision, audible voice, etc.) then that's just his fault. God is intentionally choosing that certain people go to hell by not providing sufficient reason for them to believe. For someone who is supposed to be the perfect communicator, god sure seems to struggle with this doing this.

God choosing that people (not just some people, the majority of people) go to hell seems to contradict with other information that we have about him. The bible says that it's god's will that none perish, but that all have eternal life. Clearly this is not the case, as god has decided that most people are going to hell.


Let's look at what each of these conclusions lead to. First, we see that hell is cruel and needless suffering that does not lead to any good. Then we see that god intentionally chooses that people go there, and he could prevent people from doing so by providing them sufficient evidence but doesn't. Putting these together, we see that god intentionally subjects the majority of people to senseless eternal suffering. This is not an all-loving being. This is some evil being of cosmic horror.
